# goldwashplants.com — full SEO audit, 13 August 2026

Measured against the live site, the built output, Google Search Console
(screenshot supplied by Bryce) and the SEMrush US database. Every number below
was pulled on 13 Aug 2026 unless dated otherwise.

---

## The short version

The site is technically clean. That is not the problem.

The problem is that **commercial search terms slid 4–8 positions between 15 July
and today**, while informational terms held or gained. SEMrush's traffic estimate
halved (144 → 68 visits/mo). Actual Search Console clicks did **not** collapse —
748 clicks over roughly three months, flat to slightly up. Both things are true,
and the difference between them is the whole story: the site still earns clicks
from long-tail and brand-adjacent queries, but it is losing the terms that
signal someone is ready to buy a wash plant.

Two defects found this session were live and doing damage. Both are fixed.

## 1. Four product/location images were 404ing — including the one Chase reported

Chase asked about the missing M100 photo on **7 August** and again on **9 August**.
He was right, and the cause was a one-word path bug.

Four `<img>` tags used a **relative** `../images/…` path on pages that live one
directory deep. `/products/100-ton/` + `../images/` resolves to
`/products/images/m100-machine.jpg`, which returns **404**. The image file itself
was present and served fine at `/images/m100-machine.jpg` (200).

| Page | Image | Status before |
|---|---|---|
| `/products/100-ton/` | `m100-machine.jpg` | 404 |
| `/products/50-ton/` | `m50-machine.jpg` | 404 |
| `/locations/alaska/` | `loading-washplant.jpg` | 404 |
| `/locations/alaska/` | `washplant-action.jpg` | 404 |

`/products/200-ton/` used an absolute `/images/…` path and was never affected —
which is why this went unnoticed.

**Fixed** in `c2a6cd2`. All four now use absolute paths, with descriptive alt text
and `loading="lazy"` to match the 200-ton pattern.

⚠️ **Watch for this pattern.** Any new page one directory deep that uses
`src="../images/…"` will break the same way. The audit script in
`/private/tmp/.../audit.py` checks for it; worth moving into the build.

## 2. The site published 145 reviews it does not have

This is the most serious finding and it had been flagged before.

Live structured data declared:

- Homepage `Organization` → `aggregateRating` **4.8 from 127 reviews**
- `/products/50-ton/` `Product` → `aggregateRating` **4.9 from 18 reviews**

The 22 July session recorded that the site has **zero reviews** — that was named
as "the biggest local handicap." The 19 May session had already flagged the
homepage rating: *"verify this is real and backed by collected reviews, or remove
it (fake rating markup is a manual-action risk)."* It was never resolved.

Self-serving `aggregateRating` markup that is not backed by reviews collected and
displayed on the page violates Google's structured data policy. The usual outcome
is not a dramatic penalty — it is Google quietly discounting the site's structured
data, and in worse cases a manual action against rich results. Search Console's
sidebar shows **Review snippets**, **Product snippets** and **Merchant listings**
reports, all of which read this markup.

The same page also carried an `Offer` declaring **price $35,000** and **$0
shipping** — on a page that publishes no price, for a company that ships
worldwide. The other three product pages deliberately carry no `Offer` for
exactly this reason (documented 19 May).

**Fixed** in `c2a6cd2`: both `aggregateRating` blocks and the 50-ton `Offer`
removed. Zero `aggregateRating` remain in the build.

🔴 **Open question for Chase.** The three homepage testimonials (Mike R. /
Fairbanks, James T. / Grass Valley, Kwame A. / Accra) are still on the page. They
are plausible and may well be real, but nobody here can confirm them. If they are
real, get those customers to leave actual Google reviews — that converts an
unverifiable claim into a ranking asset. If they are not, they should come down.

## 5. The 293 "not indexed" pages are mostly legacy, and mostly benign

Search Console reports **46 indexed / 293 not indexed**. That looks alarming and
mostly is not.

The site publishes **57 pages** (56 in the sitemap, `/hq/` correctly excluded and
noindexed). So ~283 of those 293 URLs are **not current site pages**. They are
almost certainly legacy URLs from the old **goldwatchproject.com** WordPress site —
tag archives, category archives, attachment pages, paginated archives. That kind of
bloat generates hundreds of URLs, and Google reporting them as "Page with redirect"
is expected, not a fault.

Sitemap health was verified directly: **all 56 URLs return 200 with no redirect
hops.** Location and blog pages were tested for near-duplication and none exceeded
70% similarity, so duplicate content is not the cause either.

**The real number to care about is 46 indexed out of 56 indexable** — roughly ten
real pages are not in the index. Identifying which ten requires the Search Console
Pages report (filter: *Crawled — currently not indexed* and *Discovered — currently
not indexed*). That is a five-minute job for whoever has GSC open.

## 6. ✅ The migration redirects — FIXED 13 Aug, and the cause was not what anyone thought

**Status: shipped and verified. All 16 legacy URLs now land on their correct
page.**

`goldwatchproject.com` had been **301ing every URL to the goldwashplants.com
homepage**. All 16 mapped URLs missed their target.

**The real cause: the work was 90% done and one step from live.** In Cloudflare's
GullStack account there was already a **Bulk Redirect List named `new_site`
containing exactly these 16 redirects** — uploaded in May, contents correct, all
301. But its status was **Inactive**, and the account had **0 Bulk Redirect
Rules**. A Bulk Redirect List does nothing on its own; it only takes effect when a
Bulk Redirect Rule references it. Nobody ever created the rule.

Two changes made it work:

1. Created Bulk Redirect Rule **"goldwatchproject legacy page redirects"** →
   `new_site`. The list flipped Inactive → Active.
2. That alone was **not** enough — the zone-level Redirect Rule "Gold Wash Plants"
   (`All incoming requests` → 301 homepage) still won. **Empirically, the
   zone-level Single Redirect beats the account-level Bulk Redirect on this zone**,
   which is the opposite of what the phase ordering suggests. Verified by testing
   one path, then all sixteen. So the catch-all's match was changed from
   *All incoming requests* to a custom expression that excludes the 16 legacy
   paths:

   ```
   not (http.request.uri.path in {"/blog" "/category/mining-knowledge"
   "/gold-wash-plant-info" "/gold-wash-plant-maintenance-101-trouble-free-mining"
   "/mining-knowledge-101-what-we-have-learned" "/tag/gold-mining" "/tag/mining"
   "/tag/trommel" "/tag/wash-plant" "/wash-plant-examples" "/wash-plant-pricing"
   "/water-needs-for-gold-processing" "/contact" "/contact.html" "/examples"
   "/examples.html"})
   ```

Verified live: **16 of 16 reach their intended target in a single hop**, and every
unmapped legacy URL (`/about/`, `/careers/`, `/cat-rentals/`, `/2017/02/`, random
paths) still falls through to the homepage as before.

⚠️ **Trailing-slash variants are deliberately not excluded.** The Bulk Redirect
list stores sources without a trailing slash and `subpathmatching` is off, so
`/blog/` would match neither rule and would hit the origin. Leaving it on the
catch-all keeps it safe. If you ever add trailing-slash sources to the list, add
them to the exclusion expression at the same time.

### 🔴 Correction to this audit's original framing

The first version of this document called the redirect map "the most valuable
unshipped work on the account" and said the blanket redirect was "throwing away"
the value of 132 referring domains. **That was overstated, and the backlink data
does not support it.**

Checked afterwards against SEMrush:

| Old URL | Referring domains | Backlinks |
|---|---|---|
| `https://goldwatchproject.com/` | **69** | 143 |
| `http://goldwatchproject.com/` | **26** | 39 |
| every other legacy URL in the top 30 | **0** | 0 |

Roughly **95 of the referring domains point at the old homepage**, which was
already redirecting homepage → homepage — a correct 1:1 redirect that passes
equity fine. The deep pages that this map fixes have **essentially no external
links at all**. The referring domains are also mostly low quality
(`analyticshaven.top`, `blogsphere.top`, `cheapsmmprovider.online` — Authority
Score 6); only `juniorminers.com` at AS 22 looks legitimate.

**So the honest value of this fix is:** real people clicking old links now reach
the right page, and Google stops seeing a pile of legacy URLs redirect to an
irrelevant homepage — a soft-404 pattern that plausibly contributes to the
"293 not indexed" count. Both are worth having, and it cost nothing. **It is not a
link-equity recovery, and it should not be sold as one.**

**The highest-return work outstanding is the cannibalization in section 3**, not
this.

## 9. Cannibalization — the actual mechanism, and the first fix

Section 3 described the symptom. The cause is measurable in the internal links.

**23 in-content links point at the homepage, and every one of them read
"gold wash plants for sale."** That term ranks **#3**. The 390/mo head term
`gold wash plant` had **no exact-match anchor support at all**, and the only two
links using that anchor pointed at `/products/`, which ranks **#44** for it.

Worse: **the homepage never used the singular phrase once in its body copy** —
0 occurrences, while every product page used it 5 times.

So Google was being told, consistently and by us, that `/products/` owns the head
term, while the page it actually ranks best (the homepage, #15) never mentioned it.

**Fixed:**
1. The homepage now explains what a gold wash plant is, in a paragraph that uses
   the phrase naturally rather than as a keyword drop.
2. `equipment-callout.njk` alternates its anchor by page URL, producing a **12/11
   split** between "gold wash plants for sale" and "gold wash plant" across the
   same 23 links. No new links were added.

This applies the mechanism that already earned a #3 ranking on this site to the
term that is worth the most. **Still to do:** the remaining six split terms, and
rewriting the competing body copy on `/how-it-works/` and `/products/`.
